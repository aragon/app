# OSx orientation coverage map

The question set for the [OSx orientation design](../osx-orientation.md): for each representative question, the short answer the entry page must support on its own, where the platform answer stops, the precise next link with its reason, and the content home. The assistant retrieves platform pages only, so every short answer must stand alone and every next link is a destination it can name but not read.

## Summary

| # | Question | Platform-only short answer | Stop here | Next link (reason) | Home |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What is OSx, and what does the platform add to it? | OSx is the protocol the platform runs on: one `DAO` contract per organization that holds assets, executes actions and owns its permission table, extended by plugins under one permission system. The platform is the application over it: product vocabulary (account, process, body), guided creation and governance design, readable actions and indexed views. It simplifies supported flows and never restricts the account. | Before the DAO contract's functions, the plugin framework's setup and registry mechanics, or any Solidity. | [Why OSx](../../../protocol-doc/guides/why-osx.md) (the no-code rationale); [The DAO contract](../../../protocol-doc/core/dao.md) and [The permission system](../../../protocol-doc/core/permissions.md) (the two ideas everything builds on). | Entry page § 2; [Account](../../../accounts/account.md), [Plugin](../../../governance/plugin.md). |
| 2 | Why does signing a DAO or governance action interact with an OSx contract? | Because the account is an OSx `DAO` contract and its governance runs in OSx plugins: creating a proposal calls the process's plugin, executing one makes the plugin call the account's `execute`, installing governance calls the plugin setup processor, creating an account calls the factory. One transaction may carry a batch of actions; the app's Basic, Decoded and Raw views are the readable route before signing. Signing submits; a proposal's actions run only after it passes and is executed. | Before wallet rendering (wallet-specific, open in `supply-wallet-batch-confirmation-evidence`) and before calldata layouts. | [Actions and execution](../../../protocol-doc/core/execution.md) (what `execute` does and why actions run as the account); [Create, vote, and execute a proposal](../../../protocol-doc/guides/create-vote-execute.md) (the lifecycle in code). | Entry page § 4; [Action](../../../governance/action.md), [Transaction submission stepper](../../../application/submitting-a-transaction.md). |
| 3 | Where does the information shown in the app come from? | From an index the platform backend builds by watching events, primarily those emitted by OSx contracts (the account, the framework, installed plugins), plus token events, balance reads, ENS records and price data where a feature needs them; the app reads that derived view. It can lag the chain, so a confirmed transaction may take a moment to appear, and the chain remains authoritative. | Before event inventories, indexer topology or synchronization behavior. | [DAO metadata](../../../protocol-doc/core/dao-metadata.md) (why metadata is read from events); [Registries and ENS names](../../../protocol-doc/framework/registries.md) (how DAOs and plugins are discovered onchain). | Entry page § 3; [Platform design principles](../../design/principles.md) rule 1, [Assets](../../../treasury/assets.md), [Permission Viewer](../../../access-control/permission-viewer.md#interpretation-boundary). |
| 4 | How do I change Token Voting settings, and when do I need protocol documentation? | Settings live in the Token Voting plugin and change through an action the account executes: compose **Update Token Voting settings** in a proposal on a process authorized to make that change, or in a direct transaction if you hold execute permission; existing proposals keep the settings recorded at their creation. Read the protocol page when you need the meaning of a threshold, mode or minimum, or the rule about which proposals a change affects. | Before parameter definitions, ratio math or contract functions. | [Token Voting](../../../protocol-doc/plugins/token-voting-plugin.md#the-proposal-lifecycle) (settings and the frozen-at-creation rule); [Majority voting](../../../protocol-doc/plugins/majority-voting.md) (the three thresholds); [Voting modes](../../../protocol-doc/plugins/voting-modes.md). | Entry page § 5; [Basic action views](../../../application/basic-action-views.md#token-governance), [Governance designer](../../../governance/governance-designer.md#editing-across-the-lifecycle), [Create transaction](../../../treasury/create-transaction.md). |
| 5 | Why can I find a contract function or prepare an action without permission to execute it? | The action builder reads a verified contract's ABI to show what the contract can do; whether your batch runs is decided at execution by the account's permission table (the process's Execute grant and its condition) and by each target's own access control. A visible action, a Basic form, a passing filter or a good simulation is help, not authorization. | Before the resolution rules of permission tiers and conditions. | [How a decision is made](../../../protocol-doc/core/permissions.md#how-a-decision-is-made); [ExecuteSelectorCondition](../../../protocol-doc/helpers/condition-library/execute-selector-condition.md) (how an allowlist gates a batch). | Entry page § 7; [Action builder](../../../application/action-builder.md#adding-actions), [Authorization and execution model](../../../access-control/authorization-and-execution.md), [Scoped authority](../../../access-control/scoped-authority.md), [Action simulation](../../../application/action-simulation.md). |
| 6 | What are permissions and conditions, and when do I need to understand them more deeply? | A permission records who may do what on which contract, and it lives on the account's own contract; a condition makes a grant depend on a rule checked at call time; ROOT is the permission to change permissions, and a healthy account holds it itself so changes go through governance. The app shows them in Permission Viewer, on a process's details page and in the designer's Permissions step. Go deeper before composing a grant, revoke or conditional grant, changing an allowlist, removing the last process, or acting on a Permission Viewer row. | Before tier resolution, wildcard rules, idempotency and condition rotation. | [The permission system](../../../protocol-doc/core/permissions.md) and [Permission conditions](../../../protocol-doc/common/permission-conditions.md) (the model); [Condition library](../../../protocol-doc/helpers/condition-library.md) (the ready-made conditions the app deploys). | Entry page § 6; [Permission Viewer](../../../access-control/permission-viewer.md), [OSx authorization paths](../../../access-control/osx-authorization-paths.md), [Scoped authority](../../../access-control/scoped-authority.md). |
| 7 | Why are `grant` and `revoke` not offered as default Basic actions, and where should I learn before using them? | Deliberate restraint: these operations rewrite who may do what on the account, so the app does not offer them in the curated list or as Basic forms; authors are expected to understand their consequences and research OSx first. They remain available by adding the account's contract address (a verified ABI exposes them as Decoded forms), by upload or by WalletConnect, and they execute only through a route the permission table admits, normally a proposal on a process that can make the account call itself. | Before argument encoding, batch semantics or condition-rotation detail. | [Manage permissions through governance](../../../protocol-doc/guides/manage-permissions.md) (grant, revoke, condition and rotation as a proposal); [Granting and revoking](../../../protocol-doc/core/permissions.md#granting-and-revoking) (idempotency and immutability rules). | [Action builder](../../../application/action-builder.md) (route and rationale), [Basic action views](../../../application/basic-action-views.md) (exclusion); entry page § 6 points there. |
| 8 | Can I misconfigure my DAO or governance even when using the application? | Yes. The protocol permits configurations the app does not offer or judge, and app validation refuses only settings that can never hold. Audited contracts and guided flows do not make a configuration sound: an unrealistic duration, an allowlist that denies everything, an unrestricted grant, removing the only unrestricted process, or ROOT left with an externally owned account are all valid onchain states. Test on a testnet first and read the cautions on the creation and designer pages. | Before protocol-level deployment checklists. | [Keep in mind](../../../protocol-doc/core/dao.md#keep-in-mind) on the DAO contract and [on permissions](../../../protocol-doc/core/permissions.md#keep-in-mind); [Where ROOT ends up](../../../protocol-doc/core/dao.md#where-root-ends-up). | Entry page § 8; cautions on [Account creation](../../../accounts/account-creation.md) and [Governance designer](../../../governance/governance-designer.md); [Removing a governance process](../../../governance/process-removal.md#removing-the-last-recognized-process), [Refuse unsatisfiable configuration](../../design/invariant-validation.md). |
| 9 | What does the audit/security statement cover, and where can I find the audits? | The OSx contracts and the governance plugins the app installs have been audited by several firms across releases, and Aragon reports that they govern billions of dollars in assets; each contract repository links its reports from its README, starting with the OSx audits folder. Audits cover the contracts, not the configuration an organization chooses. | Before itemizing firms, reports, versions or scope. | [Why OSx § Change, always controlled](../../../protocol-doc/guides/why-osx.md#change-always-controlled) (the protocol's own security framing); [OSx audits folder](https://github.com/aragon/osx/tree/main/audits). | Entry page § 9; [Source repositories](../repositories.md) for the contract repositories. |
